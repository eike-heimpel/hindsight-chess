# Vision

> One concept per file; they interlock but evolve independently.

## What this is

The place you go **after** you play chess — to see how you really did, relive the
turning points, and understand what happened, in plain language. Not an engine.
Not a grader. A home.

## North star: "Strava for chess"

Strava isn't where you run — it's where you go afterward to see how the run went,
watch your streaks, feel like an athlete, and occasionally train seriously. Most
users never "train"; they come for the identity and the story. A motivated subset
goes deep and pays.

That's the model. People play on chess.com / lichess. **We own the moment after
the game** — for everyone, casual and improver alike. Improvement is _one_ reason
to be here; fun, identity, and curiosity are equally valid.

## The nested model

The three things we could lead with aren't rivals — they sit at different
altitudes and stack. The north star is the **tiebreaker** when they conflict.

```
Strava for chess          ← SHAPE   (north star: optimize for the habit / the home)
   └ filled by
      "see how you played" ← HOOK    (the per-game story; the reason to open the app)
         └ made true by
            the honest coach ← MOAT  (the depth the motivated pay for; defensibility)
```

- **Shape — the home.** A beautiful, fast, free post-game dashboard you return to
  after every game. Habit and delight first, because at the beginner band the
  binding constraint is _coming back_, not depth.
- **Hook — the story.** Every game becomes a narrative: the turning point, the
  moment you lost (or saved) it, in plain English. This is the activation "whoa"
  and the share-worthy moment. It is the content that fills the home.
- **Moat — the honest coach.** Grade the player against _a better version of
  themselves_, not against Stockfish. Surface only human-relevant mistakes (the
  ones a person could realistically have found), never engine-only spikes. This is
  why the story rings true, what the motivated subset pays for, and what makes the
  product acquirable.

## The one idea that is the brand

> **We grade you against a better you, not against a machine.**

Existing features already embody it: the **blunder run-through** (worst-first, by
_sustained_ loss, excluding tactics you'd never find over the board) and
**winnable losses** ("games you should have won," gated on holding an advantage
across several of your _own_ moves). Keep that philosophy sacred.

## What we are not

- Not an engine or an analysis board — we don't compete on depth of calculation.
- Not a grader that just hands you accuracy % and a verdict. (At the beginner
  band, raw accuracy numbers are mostly noise, even demotivating.)
- Not homework. Improvement is offered, never imposed.
